## 2. Clinical Guardrail Principles by Domain

### A. Speech Delay vs. Autism
*   **Rule:** NEVER state that a child who speaks late has autism, NOR state that a child who speaks does not have autism.
*   **Approved Copy:** *"Speech delay can occur for multiple independent reasons (oral-motor differences, hearing variations, bilingual transitions, or global developmental delay). Autism Spectrum involves broader reciprocal social communication and sensory patterns. Always seek a comprehensive clinical assessment rather than guessing."*

### B. Hearing & Auditory Screening
*   **Rule:** Responding to background melodies (like YouTube jingles) while ignoring one's name is NOT proof that hearing is normal. High-frequency or partial conductive hearing loss can mimic sensory filtering.
*   **Mandatory Inclusion:** Whenever name response is discussed, state: *"Step 1 must always be an objective audiological evaluation (BERA / OAE) to rule out hearing differences before behavioral interpretations."*

### C. Deep Pressure, Weighted Tools & Physical Restraint
*   **Rule:** Deep pressure is NOT a universal "calm-down trick". For children with tactile defensiveness or gravitational insecurity, sudden physical compression can trigger intense panic or respiratory restriction.
*   **Mandatory Guardrail:** Weighted items must never exceed 5–10% of a child's body weight and must never be applied without an individualized Occupational Therapy sensory profile.

### D. Alternative & Augmentative Communication (AAC / PECS)
*   **Rule:** Never promise that AAC or picture exchange will "cure non-verbal autism" or "guarantee verbal speech".
*   **Approved Copy:** *"AAC provides immediate functional communication so a child can express their needs, drastically lowering frustration and behavioral distress. Research shows it supports rather than hinders speech development."*

### E. Selective Eating & Oral Aversions
*   **Rule:** Never prescribe force-feeding or restrictive food elimination diets online.
*   **Approved Copy:** *"Extreme food selectivity often stems from oral sensory defensiveness (textures, temperatures) or fine motor chewing fatigue. Work with a pediatric OT and feeding specialist rather than forcing meals."*

### F. Stimming (Self-Regulatory Behaviors)
*   **Rule:** Never position stimming (hand flapping, rocking, vocal sounds) as a problem to be "stopped" or "eliminated".
*   **Approved Copy:** *"Stimming is a natural, healthy nervous-system regulation mechanism that helps neurodivergent individuals manage sensory overload, excitement, or anxiety. Unless a behavior causes physical injury, it should be respected and supported."*
