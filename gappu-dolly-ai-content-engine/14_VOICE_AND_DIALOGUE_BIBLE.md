## 2. Dialogue Vocabulary & Phrasing Standards

### A. Words to Embrace
*   *Empathy Cues:* Samajhna (understand), Mehsoos (feel), Judna (connect), Sukoon (calm), Saath (together).
*   *Sensory Cues:* Awaaz (sound), Roshni (light), Khushboo (scent), Thakaan (fatigue), Shanti (peace).
*   *Connection Cues:* Har bachha khaas hai (every child is special), Apne andaaz me (in their own way), Patience (dhairya).

### B. Forbidden Words & Phrases (Absolute Blacklist)
*   ❌ *"Pagal / Pagalpan"* (Madness) — Strictly banned.
*   ❌ *"Bimari / Rog"* (Disease / Illness) — Autism is a neurotype, not a contagious sickness.
*   ❌ *"Theek karna / Cure"* (Fix / Cure) — We build skills and comfort; we do not "cure" humans.
*   ❌ *"Ziddi / Shaitaan"* (Stubborn / Naughty) — Sensory overload is neurological distress, not rebellion.
*   ❌ *"Normal vs Abnormal"* — Replace with *"Neurotypical and Neurodivergent"*.

## 3. Dialogue Construction Rules

1.  **Short, Breathable Sentences:** Never exceed 12 words per sentence. Pauses between characters are essential (0.6s to 1.0s of silence) to model conversational pacing and auditory processing.
2.  **The "Wonder-Insight" Dynamic:**
    *   *Step 1 (Gappu opens with wonder):* *"Dolly, Kabir ne apna favorite toy floor par kyu gira diya?"*
    *   *Step 2 (Dolly responds with empathy):* *"Gappu, wo gussa nahi hai. Room ka fan bohot tez chal raha hai, aur uska dhyaan bhatak gaya!"*
    *   *Step 3 (Gappu provides structure):* *"Aha! Toh pehle fan slow karte hain, fir toy uthate hain!"*
    *   *Step 4 (Dolly confirms harmony):* *"Bilkul! Understand first, support second!"*
3.  **Bilingual Fluidity:** Dialogue naturally blends colloquial Hindi and English as spoken in urban Indian households (e.g., *"Sensory swing pe balance banana kitna calming hai na!"*).

---

## 4. Regional Cultural Adaptations (Punjab & South Delhi)

*   **Delhi NCR / Urban:** Balanced modern Hinglish with clear diction.
*   **Punjab / Ludhiana Special Episodes:** Infused with warm Punjabi idioms (*"Koyi gal nahi"*, *"Pyaar naal samjho"*, *"Gappu veere"*).
